Rights

The charge was kidnapping. It was also the destruction of a right to live, to move, to keep a family, to learn, to vote, to hold what one built, and to take part in what a society makes of itself. 21 rights are named here, each tied to the instrument that recognises it — or marked as recognised by none.

1 is unenumerated: the interest a person has in their own unrealised work. No instrument protects it. The book records that as a gap in the instruments, not as a right it can assert on anyone's behalf.

Read this first

Most of these instruments postdate most of these entries, and none applies retroactively. The Universal Declaration is 1948; the Covenants are 1966; the Declaration on the Right to Development is 1986 and the United States voted against it; the Basic Principles on Reparation are 2005.

A right named against an entry therefore characterises the conduct, exactly as offense[] does. It is not an assertion that a remedy was available at the time, and nothing here should be presented to a forum as though it were. The book gains nothing by overstating this and loses the standing that makes the rest of it usable.

What the catalogue does show is which interests the conduct destroyed that the domestic constitutional catalogue has never recognised at all — 13 of the 20 enumerated rights here have no U.S. constitutional analogue, including education, health, property against arbitrary deprivation, and participation in cultural and scientific life.

To become what one might have become 22 entries

no instrument recognises this

NO positive instrument recognises this. It is a moral claim, recorded here as one and marked so. It names the interest a person has in their own unrealised work - what they would have thought, made, built or discovered - and it is the interest that foreclosed_contribution records the destruction of. The nearest thing in law is UDHR art. 27, which protects participation in cultural and scientific life but does not reach the loss of a contribution never made because the contributor was killed. The book states this as a gap in the instruments, not as a right it can assert on anyone's behalf.

What was foreclosed

The interest that no instrument protects: what a person would have made. 24 entries record its destruction. Each states the input that was destroyed, which is a fact, and none states the output, which is unknowable. That line is enforced by the validator, not by discipline — an entry claiming to know what would have been produced fails the build.

This is not modesty. A counterfactual asserted as evidence would be indistinguishable from the fabrications this book's entire evidentiary standard exists to exclude, and the first reader to notice would be right to discard everything else with it. The unknowability is also the sharper claim: it is not possible to say what was lost, and that is what makes the loss total rather than measurable.

The anti-literacy statutes: making the teaching of a child a crime

Every enslaved child in the enacting states, for 125 years, kept from literacy by criminal statute — and free Black children in several of those states. The input destroyed is a whole population's access to written thought across five generations.

Chattel enslavement in the United States and its colonial predecessors

Approximately 3.9 million people at the 1860 census alone, whose working and creative lives were appropriated in their entirety. Whatever any of them would otherwise have made was foreclosed by the same act that appropriated the labour.

The domestic slave trade: trafficking and forced displacement, 1808-1865

Approximately one million people relocated by force, and the households, apprenticeships and communities each removal dissolved.

The Clotilda: an Atlantic voyage that was a capital crime when it was made

Roughly 110 people, taken as adults and children from a place with its own language, craft and learning, none of which survived the passage intact.

The 1838 Jesuit sale of 272 people to Louisiana

272 people's labour and lineage, converted into the solvency of a college none of them could attend.

Kidnapping of Solomon Northup and the failure of the prosecution

Twelve years of one man's life, and the narrative he wrote afterwards is the only reason any of it is recoverable.

Colfax massacre and the reversal of the convictions

Between 60 and 150 people, most killed after surrender, in a parish where Black men had just been elected to office.

Killing of Thomas Moss, Calvin McDowell, and William Stewart

Three men and a going concern. Thomas Moss was a letter carrier who had built a cooperative grocery; the business was sold at a fraction of its value to the competitor whose hostility produced the killings.

Destruction of the Memphis Free Speech and banishment of its editor

A newspaper, its press, and the investigative work its editor would have done in Memphis over the following four decades had she been able to return.

Wilmington massacre and overthrow of the municipal government

A Black professional and political class — officeholders, a daily newspaper, businesses — that did not re-form in Wilmington, and 60 to 300 lives.

Killing of Mary Turner and the Brooks-Lowndes lynchings

Eleven lives at minimum, including a woman of about twenty-one and a child at term, and several hundred people who left the two counties.

Elaine massacre and the capital convictions of the survivors

Between 100 and 240 lives, and the farmers' union the meeting had been called to form.

Ocoee election day massacre

July Perry, who was organising voter registration, and a community erased from the town.

Destruction of the Greenwood District, Tulsa

Thirty-five blocks of accumulated commercial capacity — businesses, a hospital, schools, churches, libraries — and 39 to 300 lives, in a district that had become a byword for what Black enterprise could build.

Destruction of Rosewood, Florida

A town, and the eight or more people killed in destroying it.

United States Public Health Service Untreated Syphilis Study at Tuskegee

623 men observed to autopsy over forty years, their wives, and children born with congenital syphilis — plus a measured and persisting withdrawal of trust in public medicine, which forecloses care for people not yet born.

Moore's Ford Bridge killings

Four lives, one of them a veteran five years returned from the war, and a pregnancy at seven months.

Groveland Four

Four young men's twenties and thirties: one killed at once, one shot in handcuffs, two imprisoned. Charles Greenlee was sixteen.

Killing of Emmett Till

One fourteen-year-old.

Compulsory and coerced sterilisation of Black women and girls

Some 7,600 people in North Carolina alone and an estimated 60,000-70,000 nationally, together with every line of descent those operations ended. This is the only entry where the foreclosure was the explicit object rather than a consequence.

Prince Edward County closes every public school for five years

Roughly 2,000 children multiplied by five years — on the order of 10,000 child-years of schooling withheld, from a single county, by appropriation decision. It is the most exactly countable foreclosure in the corpus.

Framing and imprisonment of Clyde Kennard

One man's admission to a college, his thirties, and his life; he died at thirty-six. The degree he applied for was awarded to nobody.

Aggregate record: 3,224 lynchings, 1889-1918

3,224 lives by the compilers' own count, which they state undercounts.

Lynching of Michael Donald, and the destruction of the organisation that did it

One nineteen-year-old, chosen because he was there.

What a repeal would actually resolve

Each impediment, and what removing it would open. Most would open nothing, and saying which is the useful part.

Denial of legal personhood repudiated

Already displaced. Nothing turns on it now except that the entries it governed have no surviving cause of action, because a claim that never accrued cannot be revived.

State action doctrine superseded

Narrowed, not removed. Its full removal would matter little now: the private actors it protected are dead. Its live effect is doctrinal - it is still cited for the proposition that the Fourteenth Amendment reaches only state conduct.

Separate but equal repudiated

Repudiated. Its residue is that no federal constitutional right to education replaced it, so the Prince Edward and anti-literacy entries violate a right that exists internationally and not domestically.

Exclusion of Black jurors operative

Removing the Batson burden of proof would change contemporary practice. It would do nothing for any entry here: those juries are long dissolved and the verdicts are final.

Coroner's finding of death at the hands of persons unknown repudiated

Gone as a practice. Its effect survives as the reason attribution is unattributed in three entries - the only official inquiry that would ever occur returned a finding designed to end inquiry.

Statute of limitations operative

THE decisive one. Repeal or tolling would reopen more of this corpus than any other single change. Murder is generally not time-barred already, which is why the criminal route is theoretically alive in the killing entries and dead in all the others. A statutory tolling provision running from the date facts became provable, rather than from the injury, is the specific reform that would matter.

Sovereign and qualified immunity operative

Abolishing qualified immunity would open civil claims against officials for conduct in the modern-era entries. It would not reach the historical ones, which are barred by limitations before immunity is ever argued.

Lapse of federal cold-case jurisdiction operative

Extending the pre-1980 cut-off would enlarge federal cold-case authority. It would change almost nothing: DOJ closes these matters because every identified subject is dead, not because the date bar excludes them. This is the impediment whose removal would be most symbolic and least effective.

Riot exclusion in property insurance operative

A legislative rescission of riot exclusions applied retroactively to the destroyed districts is a live and specific proposal. It is the narrowest reform in this list with a real prospect of reaching a documented loss.

Absence of descendant standing operative

The bar that makes reparative claims legislative rather than judicial. Congress or a state legislature can create a statutory cause of action with standing for descendants; no court can. Every material restoration recorded in this book came through that route or through an institution acting on itself.
