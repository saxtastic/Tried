Post Traumatic Slave Syndrome: America's Legacy of Enduring Injury and Healing
Joy DeGruy (published as Joy DeGruy Leary), 2005
The framework for generational harm this record book had no field for until it was added. DeGruy argues that multigenerational trauma from chattel slavery and continuing oppression, without opportunity to heal, produces adaptive survival behaviours - she names vacant esteem, ever-present anger, and racist socialisation. IMPORTANT: PTSS is an influential theoretical and clinical framework, widely taught and used in practice; it is not a diagnostic category in the DSM and parts of the intergenerational-transmission literature it draws on remain contested. Slave manifests of the coastwise trade (Records of the U.S. Customs Service, Record Group 36)
The Act of 2 March 1807, which ended the international trade from 1808, required a manifest for every enslaved person shipped coastwise: name, age, sex, height, owner, port of departure, port of arrival. The federal government therefore REQUIRED, collected and preserved a passenger record of the domestic trafficking it did not prohibit. These manifests name tens of thousands of individuals and are among the largest bodies of individuating evidence in the entire corpus. 		U.S. National Archives and Records Administration public domain 2
County deed, probate and chattel-mortgage books
Bills of sale, estate inventories, division-of-property decrees, and mortgages pledging enslaved people as collateral. Recorded in the public land and probate books because title had to be provable against third parties. The conduct is documented in the transacting party's own hand, under oath or seal, and survives in volume no narrative corpus can match. 		County clerks and state archives, the several slaveholding states mixed 1
Slave-trading firm records, incl. Franklin & Armfield
Franklin & Armfield of Alexandria was the largest firm in the domestic trade, shipping enslaved people coastwise to Natchez and New Orleans through the 1830s. Firm ledgers, correspondence and shipping records document the trade as a business: volumes, routes, prices, and the deliberate targeting of the Upper South as a supply region. 		Various; substantial holdings at the Library of Congress, Chicago History Museum, and state archives mixed 1
Slavery-era insurance policy registries
Produced under state slavery-era insurance disclosure acts, which required insurers still doing business in those states to search their archives and report policies written on the lives of enslaved people. Names insureds, slaveholders, and underwriters. A record of the trade created by its financiers and surfaced by statute a century and a half later. 		California Department of Insurance; Illinois Department of Insurance public domain —
Georgetown Slavery Archive
Documents the 1838 sale of 272 enslaved people by the Maryland Province of the Society of Jesus. The articles of agreement, the ship manifests, and the correspondence are the institution's own records, and they name the people sold - which is why descendants can be identified today, and why this is one of the few entries in this book with a living, named descendant community.
