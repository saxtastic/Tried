LEG-BLACK-CODES

Black Codes and vagrancy statutes

Mississippi Black Code (1865) and successors across the former Confederate states · 1865-1866

explicit documented Freedpeople

Effect

Criminalised unemployment, 'vagrancy', breaking a labour contract, and assembling after dark; imposed apprenticeship of Black minors to former owners.

Mechanism

The statutes named the class directly in their early forms and, where amended to appear neutral, defined offences - vagrancy, contract-breaking - that only a landless labouring population could commit. Conviction then supplied labour to the convict lease.

How the text produced the result. Required on every entry: without it this would be an accusation rather than a record.

Since

Displaced by the Civil Rights Act of 1866, the Fourteenth Amendment, and Reconstruction military government; substantially revived after 1877 in altered form.

Sources: SRC-NARA-KKK-HEARINGS, SRC-FREEDMENS-BUREAU, SRC-CHRONICLING-AMERICA

LEG-PUNISHMENT-CLAUSE

The punishment clause of the Thirteenth Amendment

U.S. Const. amend. XIII, § 1 · 1865

explicit documented still operative Convicted persons; in practice, disproportionately Black men in the former Confederate states

Effect

Abolished slavery 'except as a punishment for crime whereof the party shall have been duly convicted', preserving involuntary servitude as a lawful sentence.

Mechanism

A single exception clause. Combined with statutes criminalising the ordinary conditions of Black life, it made conviction the lawful route back to compelled labour, and convict leasing supplied state revenue for decades.

How the text produced the result. Required on every entry: without it this would be an accusation rather than a record.

Since

Never amended. Several states have removed analogous exceptions from their own constitutions since 2018.

Sources: SRC-US-REPORTS, SRC-CHRONICLING-AMERICA

LEG-DISFRANCHISEMENT

The disfranchising constitutions

Mississippi Constitution of 1890; South Carolina 1895; Louisiana 1898; North Carolina 1900; Alabama 1901; Virginia 1902; Georgia 1908 · 1890-1908

facially neutral documented Black voters in the former Confederate states

Effect

Poll taxes, literacy and understanding tests, property requirements, and grandfather clauses removed Black voters from the rolls almost entirely. Louisiana's Black registration fell from roughly 130,000 in 1896 to about 1,300 in 1904.

Mechanism

The texts named no race. They tested for literacy in a population that anti-literacy statutes had spent a century and a half preventing from becoming literate, gave registrars an unreviewable 'understanding' discretion, and exempted white voters through grandfather clauses tied to 1867 enfranchisement. The mechanism was to test for a deficit the same legislatures had created.

How the text produced the result. Required on every entry: without it this would be an accusation rather than a record.

Since

Grandfather clauses struck in Guinn v. United States (1915); poll taxes by U.S. Const. amend. XXIV (1964) and Harper (1966); literacy tests suspended by the Voting Rights Act of 1965. The VRA's preclearance coverage formula was struck down in Shelby County v. Holder (2013).

LEG-SSA-NLRA-EXCLUSIONS

Occupational exclusions in the Social Security Act and the National Labor Relations Act

Social Security Act of 1935, Pub. L. 74-271; National Labor Relations Act of 1935, Pub. L. 74-198 · 1935

facially neutral documented Agricultural and domestic workers

Effect

Both statutes excluded agricultural and domestic workers from their coverage - from old-age insurance and unemployment compensation, and from the right to organise and bargain collectively.

Mechanism

Occupational categories, not racial ones. But roughly three in five Black workers nationally, and a substantially higher share in the South, worked in exactly those two categories. The exclusion was demanded by Southern congressional delegations as the price of passage, so the categories were selected knowing what they contained. This is the canonical instance in American law of a facially neutral classification chosen for a known racial result.

How the text produced the result. Required on every entry: without it this would be an accusation rather than a record.

Since

Social Security coverage extended to most agricultural and domestic workers in stages from 1950. Agricultural workers remain excluded from the NLRA to this day.

Sources: SRC-NARA-RG60, SRC-CHRONICLING-AMERICA

LEG-FHA-REDLINING

Federal mortgage underwriting standards

National Housing Act of 1934, Pub. L. 73-479; FHA Underwriting Manual; Home Owners' Loan Corporation appraisal maps · 1934-1968

explicit documented Black homebuyers and Black neighbourhoods

Effect

Federally insured mortgage credit was systematically withheld from Black neighbourhoods and from Black buyers in white ones, across the period in which federal insurance created the modern American middle class through homeownership.

Mechanism

Underwriting standards, not statute. The FHA's own manual instructed appraisers that neighbourhoods should be protected from 'inharmonious racial groups' and recommended restrictive covenants; HOLC maps graded neighbourhoods with Black residents as hazardous. The effect compounds, because home equity is the principal vehicle of inherited wealth in the United States.

How the text produced the result. Required on every entry: without it this would be an accusation rather than a record.

Since

Racial covenants made unenforceable by Shelley v. Kraemer (1948); the practice prohibited by the Fair Housing Act of 1968. The resulting distribution of home equity was not disturbed.

Sources: SRC-NARA-RG60, SRC-NMAAHC

LEG-GI-BILL

Servicemen's Readjustment Act, as locally administered

Servicemen's Readjustment Act of 1944, Pub. L. 78-346 · 1944

facially neutral documented Black veterans of the Second World War

Effect

The largest programme of publicly funded upward mobility in American history reached Black veterans at a fraction of the rate it reached white ones.

Mechanism

The statute's text is race-neutral and its administration was devolved to states, localities, banks and universities. Mortgage guarantees required a willing lender in a market shaped by FHA underwriting; tuition benefits required an admitting institution in states where universities were segregated by law and Northern ones operated quotas. The mechanism is delegation: a neutral entitlement administered through segregated institutions produces a segregated result without a single discriminatory word in the statute.

How the text produced the result. Required on every entry: without it this would be an accusation rather than a record.

Since

Superseded by later veterans' legislation. No compensating provision was ever enacted.

Sources: SRC-NARA-RG60, SRC-BEHIND-THE-VEIL

LEG-CRACK-POWDER

The 100:1 crack and powder cocaine sentencing ratio

Anti-Drug Abuse Act of 1986, Pub. L. 99-570 · 1986

facially neutral documented still operative Black defendants in federal drug prosecutions

Effect

Five grams of crack cocaine triggered the same five-year mandatory minimum as five hundred grams of powder. The overwhelming majority of federal crack defendants were Black; the majority of powder defendants were not.

Mechanism

The statute distinguishes two forms of one chemical. The distinction tracked the form each population used, so a hundredfold sentencing difference attached to a difference in preparation rather than in conduct or quantity of the active drug. The United States Sentencing Commission reported the disparity to Congress repeatedly from 1995 and recommended equalisation; Congress declined for a further fifteen years.

How the text produced the result. Required on every entry: without it this would be an accusation rather than a record.

Since

Reduced from 100:1 to 18:1 by the Fair Sentencing Act of 2010, Pub. L. 111-220; made retroactive by the First Step Act of 2018, Pub. L. 115-391. A disparity of 18:1 remains in force.

Sources: SRC-NRE, SRC-NARA-RG60

LEG-1994-CRIME-BILL

Violent Crime Control and Law Enforcement Act

Pub. L. 103-322 · 1994

facially neutral contested still operative Contested; see mechanism

Effect

Federal three-strikes provisions, an expanded federal death penalty, and grants conditioned on truth-in-sentencing laws requiring inmates to serve 85 per cent of a sentence.

Mechanism

The truth-in-sentencing incentive is the clearest channel: federal prison-construction money was conditioned on states adopting the rule, and many did. But the causal share is genuinely disputed. Most incarceration growth was at state level and much of it predates 1994; a substantial scholarly literature holds that the Act accelerated a trend rather than creating one, and another holds its effect has been overstated relative to state sentencing changes and prosecutorial charging.

How the text produced the result. Required on every entry: without it this would be an accusation rather than a record.

Since

Partially modified by the First Step Act of 2018.

Recorded as contested rather than documented because the literature is in open disagreement about causal share. Including it at 'documented' would be the same error the book refuses elsewhere: adopting a contested finding because it points the expected way.

Sources: SRC-NRE

LEG-CONTEMPORARY-FEDERAL-WORKFORCE

Contemporary federal employment and programme changes affecting Black women [UNVERIFIED]

Not established. No statute, executive order or rule is identified in this entry. · asserted 2025-2026

unstated asserted not verified Black women in federal and federally funded employment

Effect

ASSERTED, NOT VERIFIED. That recent federal legislation and administrative action has produced disproportionate job loss among Black women.

Mechanism

The STRUCTURAL EXPOSURE is documented and is what makes the claim plausible enough to be worth testing: Black women have been over-represented in federal civilian employment relative to their share of the overall labour force for decades, public employment has been a principal route into the Black middle class, and public-sector contraction has previously fallen disproportionately on Black workers - the pattern was documented extensively during the post-2008 austerity period. Black women also complete bachelor's degrees at higher rates than Black men. None of that establishes the asserted effect; it establishes only that the exposure exists and that the mechanism would be occupational concentration rather than any racial term.
