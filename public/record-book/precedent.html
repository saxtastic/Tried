The claim under test

That reparation is refused as a matter of principle, not prevented as a matter of capacity. This page does not argue it. It computes it, on every build, from the programmes below, and it is built so it can fail: if any enacted programme had required an instrument that did not already exist, the line beneath would say so.

11 of 12 programmes on file were enacted and executed (10 governmental). 0 required machinery that did not already exist. Every one reused ordinary appropriation, claims, settlement or conveyance machinery — the same instruments a legislature uses on any ordinary day. On this evidence the residual obstacle is a decision.

This is a finding about 11 programmes, not a prediction about the next one. Scale, class definition and political cost are real questions and are not answered here. What is answered is narrower and load-bearing: the machinery exists, and has been used.

Who was paid

9 of 11 enacted programmes ran to the harmed. 2 ran to the holders of title: PRE-1837-UK-SLAVE-COMPENSATION, PRE-1862-DC-COMPENSATED-EMANCIPATION.

Compensation for slavery has already been legislated, appropriated and delivered in the Anglo-American tradition — to the party holding title, within a year of enactment, on both sides of the Atlantic. The identification problem, the valuation problem and the administration problem were all solved. They were solved for the owners.

The control: Tulsa Race Riot Commission recommendations, not enacted

The control case, and the reason this file exists. A state commission found the facts and recommended payment; what failed was not identification, not valuation, and not administration. It is entered so that the feasibility claim can be tested against a refusal rather than only against successes.

The programme was never enacted, so no machinery was tested. The commission established the facts; the legislature declined the remedy; the federal suit was dismissed as time-barred.

Delivery

The enslaved received nothing and were placed under apprenticeship. The public debt raised to pay the owners was reported by HM Treasury as finally discharged in 2015.

Civil Liberties Act of 1988

Exclusion and mass incarceration of Japanese Americans during the Second World War

Jurisdiction
United States, federal · statute, 1988-08-10
Class
Surviving persons of Japanese ancestry who were interned, excluded or relocated
Beneficiary
the harmed — reached the harmed: yes
Amount
$20,000 per person; on the order of 82,000 payments and approximately $1.6 billion (figures approximate)
Delivery
An Office of Redress Administration in the Department of Justice identified eligible people from wartime government records and paid them, with a formal apology, largely without requiring an application
Machinery
existing — An appropriation, an administrative office, and the government's own records used to find the claimants. The identification problem that is said to make reparation impossible was solved by reading the files the state had made while doing the harm.
Enacted to first payment
About 26 months
Note
The central existence proof. A class defined by a historical wrong, located from administrative records, paid at national scale, within a presidential term.
