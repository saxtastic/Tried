# The single-page workstation: where the interface and the practice diverge

Written before anything is built, because the last four gaps are reasons the
obvious design fails, and building it first would have hidden them.

## The unit is wrong

A swipe card carries a yes/no. Yes/no triages; it does not execute. Sorting
twenty-five domains into keep and discard produces a shorter list and no work.

The unit that produces work is a **decision plus the small set of questions that
converts a yes into a draft**. Three outcomes, not two:

| Gesture | Meaning | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| left | no | the reason is recorded, and the card does not return |
| down | not now | it returns when its decay window says so, not tomorrow |
| right | yes — ask me | the question set opens. Answering it produces the artifact |

Right is not "accept". Right is "interview me now". That is the only gesture
that ends in something shippable, and it is the one the binary model has no
room for.

## Decay, not domain, orders the queue

Day trading decays in minutes. A fellowship deadline decays on a fixed date. A
property analysis decays over a quarter. Family archive does not decay at all.

Ordered by domain, the fast items bury the slow ones and the slow ones make the
fast ones feel unurgent. Ordered by decay, the queue answers one question —
*what stops being possible soonest* — and everything else is correctly absent.

## The question sets are the asset, not the interface

Each domain has a small fixed set that turns a yes into a draft: tone, register,
constraints, budget, materials, what you are worried about. Five or fewer, or
the interview costs more than writing the thing.

Those sets do not exist yet. They are cheap to write and they are the entire
difference between a dashboard and a to-do list with animation. Nothing else
here is hard.

## Overwhelm is subtraction, not layout

Twenty-five domains on one page is twenty-five domains on one page in any
typography. Collapsing them into accordions moves the overwhelm without
reducing it.

The page shows the five items that decay soonest. Everything else is not
collapsed, not greyed, not behind a tab — it is **not rendered**. It surfaces
when its decay window opens. A workstation that shows everything it knows is a
filing cabinet with a login.

## A refusal with no reason teaches nothing

If left-swipe records only "no", the same card returns next week and the fit
criteria never sharpen. The reason is the training signal: wrong register, bad
timing, below the money floor, not the kind of room I want to be in. Six reasons
cover almost everything, and picking one is one more tap.

## The claims in the output are still claims

"Cite the Wired piece the news agent surfaced, because it will catch this
person's attention" is a product claim in the sense P9 already means: the
citation needs the record behind it, and the assertion about what catches
someone's attention is an inference and should read as one. A draft that mixes
a sourced article with an invented assumption about a reader is the same defect
this repository already found in sixteen fellowship records.

## What is unresolved

- The domain list here was transcribed from speech and is a DRAFT. It has not
  been confirmed, and several entries may be one domain or three.
- Nothing establishes which domains the operator actually wants interviewed
  versus handled silently. Some of this should never surface as a card.
- Whether the record-book's METHODOLOGY.md becomes the shared touch point for
  all of it, or stays scoped to that instrument, is undecided and is the only
  existing candidate.
